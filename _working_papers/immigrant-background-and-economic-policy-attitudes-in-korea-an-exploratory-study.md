This exploratory study examines whether immigrant background is associated with economic policy attitudes in South Korea. It began as a paper for a Research Methods for Political Science and International Relations 2 course that I audited, motivated by an interest in identity and potential political cleavages.

The analysis uses the Korean General Social Survey (KGSS), which began recording parents’ nationality in 2016. Across the period examined, fewer than 50 respondents had foreign-born parents. This measure provides a limited proxy for immigrant background, and the small subgroup sample constrains the analysis.

Initial comparisons covered political, social, and economic attitudes. The draft focuses on economic attitudes, where some variation was observed; this focus followed the initial comparisons and should be treated as exploratory. The results suggest a possible preference for greater welfare provision among respondents with immigrant backgrounds, but do not establish a robust general pattern.
